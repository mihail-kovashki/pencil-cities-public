How it was made

Seoul in Pencil is central Seoul drawn as one architectural pencil sheet you can turn, slice and walk through. Every line on it is drawn by code from real data about the city. None of it is a photo, and none of it is an AI-generated image.

16sheets, from Gyeonghuigung to Dongdaemun and Naksan
~11,600buildings, each with its own floors
1ink, on white or graph paper

Where the shapes come from

  • Streets and building outlines: OpenStreetMap, the map that volunteers draw, as packaged by the Overture Maps Foundation. For a few dozen buildings, the outlines come from an open research dataset of East Asian buildings (Shi et al. 2023).
  • Floors and heights: the building register of Korea's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, which records each building's floors, and Seoul's own 3D map, S-Map. Where neither has a number, the floor count is estimated.
  • The ground: Seoul's survey heights from S-Map, so streets climb the real hills around Naksan and Bukchon.

What is done by hand

  • The sheets' borders: each sheet is outlined along streets and walls, never through a building, and checked against satellite pictures.
  • Landmarks: more than 40 of them, from palace halls, city gates and the city wall to City Hall, Myeongdong Cathedral and Dongdaemun Design Plaza, are modelled one by one from photographs, so they read as themselves. A landmark modelled without good photos says so on its card ("provisional").
  • Corrections: where the open data is wrong, a kerb in the wrong place or a missing building, it is fixed from satellite and street photos, one place at a time.

The photos are only used as reference. None of them appears on the map.

How it is drawn

The map is a single web page that draws in your browser with WebGL, using the three.js library. Each building is cut into its real floors, so you can lift floors away or show only the lower ones. Edges, hatching and the faint construction lines are drawn as lines, in the style of a blue pencil drawing on white paper. The sun crosses the sky through the day and the seasons and casts soft shadows.

The cars, buses and people are a simulation. They follow the drawn lanes, signals and crossings to bring the streets to life. They are a style, not a record of real traffic.

Who made it

Seoul in Pencil is a personal project by MiKo, who chose the places, the look and every change. The code was written with AI coding assistants under MiKo's direction, and every version is checked before it goes live. No AI image generator was used. The drawing is the code's output from the data above.
